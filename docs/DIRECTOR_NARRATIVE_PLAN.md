# Nereis Hero — Director Narrative Plan

> Status: **proposed production structure**
>
> This is a director-level planning document. It organizes the confirmed canon into a playable dramatic structure without locking unresolved details prematurely.
>
> For hard canon, see [DEVELOPER_STORY_MEMORY.md](./DEVELOPER_STORY_MEMORY.md).

---

## 1. Director's Core Statement

The player is not primarily playing a knight who saves a kingdom.

The player is playing a wounded human being who:

1. loses his past,
2. receives a second life from a girl,
3. slowly learns that the world around them is larger and stranger than either of them understands,
4. falls in love,
5. and eventually discovers that intimacy itself has become the doorway to catastrophe.

The political story is the visible story.

The relationship is the emotional story.

The cosmic story is the hidden story.

The best version of the game makes those three stories become the **same story** near the end.

---

# 2. Act Structure

## Act 0 — The Broken Knight

### Player experience

The player begins close to the Hero's body, not close to the world's mythology.

The opening priority is:

**survival > confusion > identity**

The Hero escapes the Church, is physically damaged, has missing memories, and eventually reaches / is found by the village.

### What the player knows

- Hero can fight.
- Hero was imprisoned.
- Someone experimented on him.
- He does not remember enough to explain why.
- The village has something unusual about it.

### What the player does not know

- Why the Church wanted him.
- What remains inside his body.
- What Mite is.
- Why some villagers recognize patterns around him.
- Why he seems strangely familiar to the village.

### Director rule

Do not explain the entire Church experiment in the opening.

The player should first wonder:

> What happened to me?

Not:

> What is the lore of the world?

---

# 3. Act I — The Girl Who Found Him

Mite becomes one of the first important people the player meets.

This section must feel comparatively warm.

Not happy-fantasy warm.

**Human warm.**

After the Church section, ordinary things should have emotional weight:

- food
- clean water
- changing clothes
- a repaired weapon
- sitting somewhere safe
- a song
- a small joke
- being asked a normal question

Mite's role is not to explain the plot.

Her role is to give Hero a reason to stay alive.

## Relationship principle

The player should first become attached to Mite through ordinary life.

The supernatural layer comes later.

## Important production rule

Mite should behave like a genuine person.

Do not constantly foreshadow her with sinister dialogue.

Her curiosity about Hero should initially be exactly that:

**curiosity.**

---

# 4. Act II — The Village and the Kingdom

The political / exploration story opens.

The Hero begins investigating:

- Church activity
- old ruins
- local disappearances
- village rumors
- rebel movement
- traces of ancient-god research

The player gradually learns:

**the village is not innocent.**

But the village is also not simply guilty.

Different people should have different reasons.

## Relationship consequence

This is the first point where the player's priorities begin to matter.

The player can:

- spend time with Mite
- investigate immediately
- chase Church clues
- follow rebels
- explore optional areas

There is no UI telling the player:

> "You are missing your romance."

The relationship progresses through lived time.

---

# 5. Act III — The First Fracture

This is where the world begins to contradict itself.

The player discovers evidence that Hero:

- was a Kingdom knight,
- was involved in the village before the current memory,
- was experimented on,
- may contain something abnormal,
- may be important to the Church for reasons beyond punishment.

Mite remains emotionally believable.

The player should still have strong reasons to trust her.

## Hidden truth layer

This is where the player can begin finding the separate side-story threads that will eventually form the multi-part 烈火天罚 artifact.

Do not announce that these are connected.

A strong player should think:

> These are four strange stories.

Only much later:

> These were four pieces of one answer.

---

# 6. Act IV — The Point of No Return

This is the most important relationship act.

The Hero and Mite have accumulated enough ordinary history that their physical relationship feels earned.

It must never feel like:

> "Now the player unlocks romance scene."

It should feel like:

> "These two people have spent enough time together that this became inevitable."

## The hidden catastrophe

Hero carries ancient-god residue from the Church's experiment.

Mite carries the dormant condition / connection that makes her an Outer-God proxy.

Their physical union creates the resonance.

At first, neither understands what happened.

There may be no immediate monster transformation.

Instead:

- Mite's dreams change.
- She starts hearing something.
- She becomes emotionally different.
- Certain symbols begin appearing around her.
- Hero begins seeing things that were not visible before.
- The village becomes subtly stranger.

The player should realize what happened **before Hero does**.

---

# 7. Act V — The Three Roads Become Inevitable

At this point the game should no longer feel like it is asking:

> Which ending do you want?

Instead:

> What has this playthrough become?

## State A — They never become one

The player missed enough relationship opportunities.

Hero and Mite lose contact.

No physical union.

No resonance.

No Outer-God vessel.

The visible political conflict continues.

This becomes:

**凡人之墓**

The player defeats the Church through the restoration / rebel conflict.

The Kingdom survives.

The cycle of corruption begins again.

---

## State B — They become one completely

The relationship developed naturally.

The player did not obtain enough hidden resistance knowledge.

The resonance fully opens.

Mite is gradually controlled.

Hero follows the path toward eternal union.

This becomes:

**永恒乐园**

The world enters the eternal dream.

The player should be left with the unsettling possibility that the paradise is genuinely beautiful.

---

## State C — They become one, but Hero remains himself

The relationship developed.

The physical union occurred.

The player has assembled enough hidden knowledge through independent side stories.

Mite loses control.

Hero understands that something is replacing the person he loves.

This becomes:

**烈火天罚**

The Church falls.

The city collapses.

The 100-cultist spectacle occurs.

Mite is encountered as an almost-unresisting final tragedy.

The player may wait.

After roughly one minute, Mite can throw herself onto Hero's weapon.

Her death triggers the final city destruction.

---

# 8. The 100-Cultist Sequence

This is intentionally a **showpiece**.

It does not need to contain deep lore.

The purpose is contrast.

Earlier in the game:

> one enemy = danger

At the end:

> one hundred enemies = noise

The player feels powerful.

Then the game removes that power fantasy with the Mite scene.

## Production opportunities

Future crowdfunding can optionally turn this into a supporter cameo system.

The safest implementation is:

- supporter-provided display name
- consent required
- fictionalized names allowed
- no feature specifically designed to target real-world individuals

---

# 9. The Mite Scene — Director's Rhythm

Do not rush this scene.

But also do not make the player repeat a three-hit execution.

The intended rhythm is:

### Phase 1 — Silence

The fight music stops.

Mite is visibly compromised.

Hero stands in front of her.

### Phase 2 — Waiting

The player has control.

Nothing requires an immediate attack.

The scene can continue breathing.

### Phase 3 — Recognition

Mite shows small signs of remaining selfhood.

No exposition monologue.

Small behavior is stronger.

### Phase 4 — Decision

The player can strike.

Or wait.

### Phase 5 — Mite chooses

After approximately 60 seconds of inactivity:

Mite moves toward Hero's weapon and ends the confrontation herself.

This is the final contradiction:

**The Outer God controls her body.  
Mite still finds one last way to help Hero.**

### Phase 6 — Aftermath

No victory celebration.

No triumphant boss-clear music.

Only:

- silence
- ash
- fire
- the broken lullaby motif
- the city beginning to die

# 10. Final Boss Architecture

## 凡人之墓

### Phase 1
Pope / High Priest — human.

### Phase 2
Pope / High Priest — ancient-god-derived monster.

### Phase 3
King — enters personally.

The King fights because he genuinely believes the Kingdom cannot survive without his intervention.

The player defeats him.

The rebels inherit the state.

The epilogue shows the beginning of another cycle.

---

## 永恒乐园

### Phase 1
Pope / High Priest — transformed.

### Phase 2
King — transformed.

They are not just stronger monsters.

Their forms should visually represent:

- institutional faith
- political survival

Both are ultimately irrelevant to the Outer God.

Hero wins while losing himself.

---

## 烈火天罚

### Phase 1
Church final authority.

### Phase 2
City collapse.

### Phase 3
100 cultists / one-hit spectacle.

### Phase 4
Mite.

### Phase 5
Death of Mite.

### Phase 6
Mutated Church forces / remaining cultists.

### Phase 7
Final detonation.

This is a catastrophe, not a victory lap.

---

# 11. Information Control

At every act, track three things:

### What Hero knows

The character's actual understanding.

### What the player thinks

The current theory built from evidence.

### What is actually true

The hidden authorial truth.

These must not be identical.

Especially:

**Hero does not know Mite's true identity.**

The player may understand it before Hero does.

---

# 12. Director's Rule for Mite

Never allow Mite to become boringly evil.

Avoid:

- evil laughs
- obvious villain speeches
- "I was using you all along"
- early declarations about the Outer God
- excessive foreshadowing

Prefer:

- genuine affection
- curiosity
- small kindness
- jealousy that feels human
- confusion
- innocent questions
- moments where she protects Hero for no cosmic reason at all

The later tragedy only works if the player remembers the earlier girl.

---

# 13. Director's Rule for the Three Endings

They should be remembered as:

### 凡人之墓
**They never become one.**

### 永恒乐园
**They become one too completely.**

### 烈火天罚
**They become one, and the union becomes a prison.**

That sentence should guide future story, level, boss and visual decisions.

---

# 14. Future Design Gates

Narrative implementation should pause for author approval at these gates:

### Gate A
Exact Hero/Mite relationship scenes.

### Gate B
Exact moment and presentation of physical union.

### Gate C
The four side stories and four-part artifact.

### Gate D
Final Church / King political history.

### Gate E
The first clear signs that the Outer God has noticed the resonance.

### Gate F
The final Mite scene dialogue / animation language.

Everything else can be developed around these anchors without locking them prematurely.
