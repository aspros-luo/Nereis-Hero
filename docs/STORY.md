# Nereis Hero — Story Foundation

## Shared universe

This game shares the world of **Nereis Lullaby / 蜜忒之歌**.

The two games do not tell the same story from the same camera. They reveal the same world from different human perspectives.

- **Nereis Lullaby** follows Mite/Nereis and the quiet village/tavern life surrounding her.
- **Nereis Hero** follows the Hero through the church, settlements, ruins, monsters, and the forces trying to understand or control the sleeping outer-god presence.

The player should not receive the complete truth early.

## The Hero

The Hero is an amnesiac knight.

At the beginning, the player believes the Hero is simply a survivor returning to a village that has suffered a supernatural incident.

As memories return, several possibilities become impossible to ignore:

1. The Hero was involved in a church experiment.
2. The experiment attempted to create or prepare a vessel.
3. The Hero survived, but survival may have changed what a human being means.
4. The church is not the only force manipulating the Hero.
5. Mite/Nereis is connected to the missing truth.

The game should preserve ambiguity long enough for the player to form their own interpretation.

## Mite / Nereis

Mite is not the playable protagonist in this game.

Her existence is encountered indirectly at first:

- songs
- memories
- village rumors
- church records
- symbols
- dreams
- NPC testimony
- distorted recollections

The player should initially be unsure whether Mite is a victim, a monster, a god, or something that makes those categories meaningless.

## Core themes

### Humanity

The Hero begins with a fragile sense of humanity.

Humanity is not a morality bar. It represents the Hero's ability to recognize other people as people while facing fear, survival pressure, manipulation, and cosmic truth.

### Memory

Memory is unreliable.

A recovered memory can be true, incomplete, manufactured, or true from someone else's perspective.

### Faith

The Church offers protection and meaning while hiding information about the forces beyond humanity.

The game should avoid making every believer evil. Ordinary believers should have understandable reasons for believing.

### Cosmic horror

The outer gods should rarely behave like ordinary villains.

The horror comes from discovering that human concepts such as justice, identity, death, and individuality may not apply to them.

## Narrative relationship with Nereis Lullaby

Some events should intentionally overlap between the two games.

The same event may have:

- a peaceful interpretation in the village story,
- a horrifying interpretation in the Hero story,
- and a third interpretation revealed only after both stories are understood.

This is a core narrative device, not optional lore.

## Opening direction

The Hero wakes near the outskirts of a familiar village.

The village appears almost ordinary.

The first contradiction is small.

Someone recognizes the Hero.

The Hero does not recognize them.

The second contradiction is worse.

The person insists they have already met the Hero many times.

The Hero's body remembers how to fight.

The Hero's mind does not remember why.

Then, somewhere beyond the village, a lullaby can be heard.

The player does not yet know who is singing.
