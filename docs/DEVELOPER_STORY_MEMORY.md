# Latest Production Clarifications — 2026-10-07

## 烈火天罚：100 人海战术

The roughly 100 cultists are deliberately a **production spectacle / bonus program**, not a required lore delivery mechanism.

They exist so the final act can temporarily become absurdly violent and cathartic after the emotional collapse of the Mite scene.

Rules:

- Keep the target scale at **100 cultists** for now.
- They do not need unique identities or lore.
- They should not be treated as 100 separate story quests.
- They can become a future crowdfunding / supporter cameo feature.
- Supporter cameo names should be used only with consent and preferably with fictionalized or chosen display names.
- Do not build a feature for targeting identifiable real people the creator dislikes.

The important contrast is:

> 100 disposable enemies = spectacle.
>
> Mite = one irreplaceable person.

That contrast is intentional.

## 烈火天罚：Mite final encounter

The Mite encounter should **not** use a repetitive "three hits to kill her" sequence.

Mite is effectively defeated before the player's final action.

She is already under overwhelming Outer-God control.

The game should create a quiet, uncomfortable pause before her death.

### Proposed director sequence

1. Hero reaches Mite after the cultist set-piece.
2. Normal combat presentation falls away.
3. Mite is effectively at 1 HP / one decisive action from death.
4. She does not fight like a conventional boss.
5. The player can wait.
6. A hidden **~60-second idle performance window** begins.
7. If the player remains inactive long enough, Mite eventually moves by herself and **throws herself toward Hero's weapon**, turning the weapon into the instrument of her death.
8. If the player attacks earlier, the same final state can resolve without forcing a long three-hit sequence.
9. The key emotional point is that Mite is both victim and participant: the Outer God controls her, but her remaining love can still produce a last act that helps Hero end her suffering.
10. After her death, the city-collapse sequence begins.

The idle performance should be inspired by the dramatic patience of late Evangelion rather than copied shot-for-shot.

The player must have enough time to realize:

> "She is not going to attack me."
>
> "She is waiting."
>
> "She wants me to understand."

The silence itself is the scene.

## Why this works

The sequence creates three layers of agency:

- **Outer God:** controls Mite's body and makes her dangerous.
- **Mite:** retains enough selfhood to choose one last action.
- **Player:** decides whether to strike or wait.

No one gets a clean victory.

## Performance direction

The final Mite scene should prefer:

- long silence
- environmental fire / ash
- small facial or body-language changes
- fragmented breathing
- very little dialogue
- the original village / lullaby motif returning in a damaged form

Avoid:

- long exposition
- melodramatic monologues
- repeated attack animations
- a conventional boss health-bar finale

The desired feeling is:

> **The player does not defeat Mite. The player witnesses the end of Mite.**

# Current Canon Override — 2026-10-07 Story Direction

> This section has higher priority than older speculative passages elsewhere in this file.

## A. Mite's knowledge

**Mite initially knows nothing about the Outer God in the human, conscious sense.**

She is selected as the earthly proxy because of her genuine purity, but she does not begin the story consciously understanding that she is an Outer-God agent.

The turning point occurs only in the middle / late game:

1. Hero and Mite become genuinely close.
2. They eventually enter a physical relationship.
3. Hero's body carries **ancient-god residue** left by the Church's experimentation.
4. During the physical union, the ancient-god residue and the Outer-God influence associated with Mite come into contact.
5. This creates a resonance / bridge.
6. The Outer God notices, reaches through that resonance, and gradually begins to influence Mite.
7. Mite's later attempts to guide Hero toward 永恒乐园 are therefore increasingly **not fully under her control**.

This is important:

> Mite does not secretly spend the entire game plotting the apocalypse.

The tragedy is that the girl Hero fell in love with becomes the medium through which the apocalypse begins.

Her later loss of control should preserve the feeling that the earlier Mite was real.

## B. Hero's ancient-god residue

The Church's experiments did not simply damage Hero psychologically.

They left a trace of ancient-god material / power inside him.

This residue is initially dormant and invisible to Hero.

It is a major reason the physical union with Mite can become the bridge between the ancient-god and Outer-God layers of the story.

This also makes the Church's earlier experimentation directly relevant to the final relationship instead of being a disconnected backstory.

## C. Relationship / romance state

The Hero-Mite relationship is **not a conventional numeric romance meter**.

Do not reduce it to "affection +10".

It is built from concrete opportunities:

- time spent together
- optional conversations
- small favors
- remembered habits
- shared locations
- village events
- missed meetings
- decisions that make Hero leave Mite behind

The player's treatment of Mite determines whether the relationship grows naturally.

### 凡人之墓

The player who rushes the visible mainline can miss the important Mite-centered side interactions.

Eventually Hero and Mite **lose contact before the physical relationship ever occurs**.

There is:

- no physical union
- no Outer-God vessel event
- no activated ancient-god / Outer-God resonance

The player can therefore finish the political conflict without ever entering the cosmic relationship tragedy.

The player should not feel as if they "failed a romance quest."

It should feel like:

> I was so focused on saving the Kingdom that I never noticed the girl I had been leaving behind.

The absence itself is the tragedy.

### 永恒乐园

The relationship progresses naturally.

Hero and Mite eventually become physically intimate.

The ancient-god residue in Hero contacts the Outer-God influence attached to Mite.

Mite begins to be influenced and guided from outside herself.

Hero, without understanding what is happening, follows her toward the eternal dream.

The relationship is therefore not "Mite planned this from the start."

It becomes a tragedy that **grows out of genuine intimacy**.

### 烈火天罚

The relationship also reaches physical union.

By this point Mite is largely unable to control the influence acting through her.

Her behavior becomes a warped mixture of:

- genuine love
- dependency
- fear of losing Hero
- Outer-God guidance
- loss of agency

Hero retains enough individuality to understand that something is terribly wrong, thanks to the hidden multi-part artifact.

The result is **a malformed love with no clean resolution**, not a heroic romance.

## D. Three ending emotional identities

### 凡人之墓
**They never become one.**

Human love is interrupted by ordinary human priorities.

The Kingdom survives.

Humanity remains trapped in its cycle.

### 永恒乐园
**They become one too completely.**

Love becomes indistinguishable from loss of individuality.

The world becomes paradise by ending separation.

### 烈火天罚
**They become one, but the union becomes a prison.**

Mite is losing control.

Hero realizes that the person he loves is disappearing inside the thing using her.

He cannot save the relationship.

He can only end it.

That is why the final act should feel like tragedy rather than victory.

## E. Final confrontation structure

The ending fights should be treated as **three different versions of the same political/cosmic climax**.

### 凡人之墓

1. Hero confronts the Church.
2. **Pope / High Priest** — human form.
3. **Phase II:** the Pope transforms through ancient-god power.
4. **Phase III:** when the Church is about to fall, the **King personally enters the battlefield**.
5. The King is not simply "the secret final villain"; he believes he must prevent the Kingdom from collapsing.
6. Hero defeats the King.
7. The rebels take control.
8. Epilogue: the new order slowly begins reproducing the old corruption.

The important emotion is:

> Hero defeats the people who destroyed the old Kingdom — and still cannot create a lasting human utopia.

### 永恒乐园

The political climax becomes corrupted by the cosmic union.

The Hero is already being used as the Outer God's vessel.

The Church leadership and the Kingdom's sovereign are both transformed.

Recommended staging:

1. Pope becomes an ancient-god-derived monster.
2. King enters as a second monstrous sovereign.
3. They fight as separate embodiments of institutional survival.
4. Hero defeats them while increasingly losing his own human identity.
5. Mite's influence draws him toward the final union.
6. The world enters the eternal dream.

The two bosses should not feel like two random monster upgrades.

They should represent:

- **Church:** "humanity must control the divine."
- **King:** "humanity must survive at any cost."

Both positions collapse when the Outer God makes the argument irrelevant.

### 烈火天罚

This should feel less like a conventional boss rush and more like the final collapse of a city.

1. **Pope / Church final authority** is defeated.
2. Hero is surrounded by a huge number of Outer-God-influenced cultists.
3. In this special late-game state, ordinary cultists can be killed with a single decisive strike.
4. Target scale: roughly one hundred enemies across the set-piece; exact count is not important.
5. Hero reaches **Mite**.
6. Mite is already almost completely controlled and reduced to **effectively 1 HP** as a dramatic representation, not necessarily a literal UI value.
7. The game gives Hero no conventional victory over her.
8. The narrative forces the killing blow.
9. Her death triggers a new wave of cultists and mutated Church personnel.
10. The remaining city becomes unsalvageable.
11. Hero chooses / is forced into the final act: detonate the city and destroy the vessel site.

The important rule is:

> **The player does not "win" by killing Mite.**
>
> The killing is the moment the player loses her.

The explosion is not a celebration.

It is the grave of the relationship.

## F. One important gameplay modification

Do not make the one-hit cultist section feel like another ordinary combat room.

It should feel like a **powerful but horrifying set-piece**:

- continuous forward pressure
- large enemy numbers
- little downtime
- music that stops behaving like a normal boss theme
- attacks that feel like execution rather than ordinary dueling
- visual degradation as the city collapses

The player should briefly feel overwhelmingly powerful.

Then the Mite scene should remove that feeling completely.

# Director's Reference Translation — What We Borrow, What We Make Our Own

The following works are reference material, not source material to reproduce.

### The Song of Saya

Useful principle:

- horror and affection can occupy the same relationship
- a character can be emotionally sincere while being fundamentally incompatible with normal human existence
- the strongest horror is often not "the monster is pretending", but "the monster's love is real"

Nereis adaptation:

**Mite's purity is genuine.** She is not a monster wearing an innocent mask. Her innocence itself is the dangerous thing.

### Bloodborne

Useful principle:

- hidden truth can be distributed across apparently unrelated side stories
- multiple partial relics can form one late-game revelation
- the player can discover a secret ending only after understanding enough of the hidden cosmology

Nereis adaptation:

**烈火天罚 requires several independent side stories and a multi-part artifact.**

Do not present it as a checklist.

### Neon Genesis Evangelion

Useful principle:

- apocalypse can be the attempted solution to loneliness and human incompleteness
- merging humanity can look like salvation while destroying individuality
- the final conflict can be about whether people choose painful individuality over painless unity

Nereis adaptation:

**永恒乐园 is the temptation to end loneliness, death and separation by ending individuality.**

### Genesis / Eden imagery

Useful principle:

- creation
- man and woman becoming one flesh
- forbidden knowledge
- mortality
- the Tree of Life
- exile from paradise
- the flaming sword guarding the way back

Nereis adaptation:

The physical union of Hero and Mite can become a literal cosmic gateway.

The "paradise" ending can be an inversion of Eden: the door to eternal life opens, but what returns through it is not human paradise.

The flaming sword becomes a visual ancestor for **烈火天罚**: fire is no longer merely punishment from above; it becomes humanity's final answer to the false paradise.

### Lovecraftian cosmic horror

Useful principle:

- the universe does not need to hate humanity
- incomprehensible beings do not need human motives
- an encounter can be terrifying precisely because humanity is not important

Nereis adaptation:

The Outer God does not need to conquer the Kingdom for a grand purpose.

A stronger horror is that the world is merely interesting.

Human suffering, love, war and civilization can be observed the way a person watches ants change direction after a stone is dropped beside them.

This should remain mostly implicit. The Outer God must not deliver a lecture explaining itself.

# Nereis Hero — Developer Story Memory / Canonical Lore

> This is a developer-facing story memory, not a player-facing codex.
> It exists so future development sessions can recover the core setting, ending structure, character relationships, and narrative rules without relying on conversation context.
>
> Priority rule: when gameplay implementation conflicts with this file, stop and revisit the story design before adding narrative code.
>
> Do not invent missing canon. If a detail is not marked as confirmed below, treat it as a design question rather than silently deciding it.

---

## 1. Project Identity

Nereis Hero is a 2D Soulslike action RPG in the shared Nereis / 蜜忒之歌 universe.

The game is the Hero-side counterpart to Nereis Lullaby / 蜜忒之歌.

- Nereis Lullaby: the player experiences the village, tavern, Mite/Nereis, desires, manipulation, and the slow transformation of ordinary life into cosmic horror.
- Nereis Hero: the player experiences the same world through an amnesiac Kingdom knight who escapes Church experimentation, is rescued by the village, gradually reconstructs his history, and becomes emotionally attached to Mite.

The two games should overlap in objects, songs, people, symbols and events, but their interpretations can differ.

---

## 2. Core Authorial Direction

The intended experience is not simply "fight the Church and stop a god."

The emotional core is:

> A wounded human meets a seemingly pure girl.
>
> The girl genuinely cares for him.
>
> The player gradually discovers that the girl is the earthly proxy of an Outer God.
>
> She is not pretending to be pure.
>
> She really is pure — but she has not inherited ordinary human morality.
>
> Hero never understands her true identity.
>
> The player therefore knows something the Hero does not.

This creates a deliberate split between:

- **Hero's emotional truth**
- **player's discovered truth**
- **cosmic truth**

The three truths do not need to become identical.

---

## 3. The Core World Truth

There are three important forces that must not be flattened into a simple good/evil triangle:

1. The Kingdom
2. The Church and its ancient-god research
3. Mite/Nereis and the Outer God

The world should also contain other kingdoms, corrupted faith users, rebels/restoration forces, ordinary villagers, merchants, wanderers, and people acting from understandable human motives.

The player should initially misunderstand the world.

The game should not explain the full truth in chronological order.

---

## 4. The Ancient Kingdom and the Church

### 4.1 The original Kingdom

The ancient Kingdom originally expanded by using true faith power.

This faith power was not initially a fraud. It was genuinely believed in, respected, and effective.

The Church became the institution that carried and interpreted this faith.

### 4.2 The corruption of the Church

Over time, the Church became increasingly hungry for power.

The Church discovered that a sleeping ancient god existed within the Kingdom's territory.

Instead of leaving it undisturbed, the Church began secret research.

The Church extracted or studied things associated with the ancient god, including:

- bodily fluids
- hair
- other physical remains or substances
- traces of its power

From this research, the Church developed a counterfeit faith-like power based on the ancient god.

This power allowed the Church to secretly become the real ruler behind the Kingdom.

### 4.3 Human experimentation

The Church did not limit the research to itself.

It captured people from different villages and settlements for experiments.

The purpose of these experiments can include:

- understanding ancient-god power
- adapting human bodies to it
- investigating vessel compatibility
- searching for ways to reproduce or control supernatural power

The exact technical mechanism of the experiments is deliberately not fully fixed yet.

Important: the Church should not be written as cartoonishly evil. Many ordinary believers can genuinely believe they are protecting the Kingdom, preserving order, or saving humanity.

The horror comes from what the institution became willing to justify.

---

## 5. Mite / Nereis

### 5.1 Identity

Mite/Nereis is an agent/proxy of an Outer God.

She is not merely a witch, cult leader, or ordinary supernatural woman.

Her actions are connected to the arrival or manifestation of an Outer God.

### 5.2 Mite's defining trait — uncorrupted purity

Mite is intended to be a **genuinely pure young woman with an innocent, unsullied outlook**, not someone pretending to be innocent.

Her purity is important precisely because it was one reason the Outer God chose her as its earthly proxy.

The production rule is:

> **"Pure" means emotionally and morally uncorrupted by ordinary human society; it does not mean naive in the sense of unintelligent.**

She can be curious, affectionate, playful, jealous, lonely, possessive, or fascinated without those emotions being framed through conventional adult social morality.

She should not be written as a conventional seductress.

**Age/production safety rule:** Mite must be clearly an adult character. The desired "少女感" is a characterization and emotional quality, not an ambiguous minor status.

### 5.3 Mite's relationship with humanity

Mite does not initially need to understand human morality in the same way as humans.

This is where her innocence becomes frightening.

She may genuinely ask:

- Why is this wrong?
- Why does this person hate me?
- Why must Hero die?
- If someone is suffering, why not remove the suffering?
- Why is making everyone happy considered terrible?

Her actions can therefore be sincere while still being catastrophic.

She should not be evil because she enjoys cruelty.

She should be dangerous because **human moral boundaries are not naturally part of her worldview**.

### 5.4 The village

In the remote village, Mite influences people indirectly.

She uses things such as:

- desires
- greed
- hope
- fear
- dreams
- wealth / treasures
- spiritual influence
- mental influence

to encourage villagers to search for a vessel and perform a summoning ritual.

The villagers should often believe they are acting on their own decisions.

This is important: Mite should usually manipulate desire rather than openly command people.

### 5.5 Mite and Hero — first meeting

The Hero's **first true personal meeting with Mite occurs near the beginning of the game**, after the Hero has escaped the Church's experiments, lost his memory, and wandered into the village.

Mite is part of the village response that saves him.

The relationship should begin naturally:

- no instant "destiny love"
- no prophecy-driven romance
- no early supernatural confession

At first, Mite approaches Hero because she is simply curious.

Hero is an abnormal person who appears wounded, confused, and strangely familiar.

Their attachment grows from ordinary interactions.

The more emotionally important they become to each other, the more horrifying the later truth becomes.

### 5.6 Critical information asymmetry

**Hero never learns Mite's true identity during the main story.**

He does not knowingly fall in love with an Outer-God proxy.

The player may gradually discover what Mite is.

This creates the core dramatic irony:

> Hero loves the girl who saved him.
>
> The player discovers that the girl who saved him may also be the reason the world is doomed.

### 5.7 Mite's feelings

The current direction is:

**Mite's feelings for Hero are genuine.**

The story should not reduce the relationship to "she was manipulating him all along."

Her larger role as the Outer God's proxy and her personal attachment to Hero are both true.

The tragedy is that the two truths can coexist.

---

## 6. Hero — Confirmed Surface History

The Hero is a Kingdom knight.

The intended chronological surface history is:

1. The Kingdom sends the Hero to investigate Mite's remote village and local abnormalities.
2. During the investigation, the Hero is defeated by Outer-God power and cultists / influenced villagers.
3. During or after this defeat, the Hero accidentally discovers part of the Church's secret research into the ancient god.
4. Because the Hero has seen something the Church cannot allow to be exposed, the Church treats him as expendable.
5. The Church captures the Hero and begins modifying / experimenting on him.
6. The Hero escapes during the modification process.
7. The Hero loses his memories.
8. The Hero wanders back into the village.
9. This is where he first truly meets Mite; she participates in rescuing him.
10. The Church and bandits eventually surround / attack the village.
11. The Hero chooses to stand up and protect the village.
12. His attempt to uncover the truth develops into the final conflict involving the Church, the rebels/restoration forces, Mite, and the Outer God.

**Important chronology clarification:**

The Hero may have entered or observed the village area during his original Kingdom investigation without personally meeting Mite.

"First encounter with the village" and "first true personal meeting with Mite" are therefore different events.

The player should not experience the chronological history in this exact order.

---

# 7. The Player's Intended Discovery Order

The game should behave like a Soulslike investigation.

The player first sees:

- an amnesiac knight
- a strange village
- hostile Church forces
- suspicious believers
- fragments of an old conflict
- a seemingly ordinary girl who rescued the Hero
- a lullaby / recurring song

The player then discovers fragments that appear contradictory.

Examples:

- Church records say the Hero died.
- A villager remembers him alive.
- Someone remembers him before the player thinks he arrived.
- An item implies the Hero served the Kingdom faithfully.
- Another item implies the Church feared him.
- Mite remembers details Hero cannot remember.
- A dream suggests Mite loved him.
- Another piece of evidence suggests Mite was using the village.

None of these should be explained immediately.

The player must build the chronology themselves.

---

# 8. The Three Canonical Endings

There is no universally good ending.

The three endings represent three different answers to the question:

> What should humanity do when it encounters something greater than humanity?

They must not be presented as a simple final-choice menu.

The ending should emerge from the player's accumulated actions, exploration, hidden knowledge, relationship with Mite, and response to supernatural power.

---

## 8.1 Ending I — 凡人之墓

### Intended player profile

Approximately 70% of normal players should reach this ending.

They play through the obvious/main progression but do not deeply investigate hidden lore, obscure side content, optional items, secret areas, or the Church/Outer-God truth.

They may never fully understand:

- the Church's ancient-god research
- the real nature of Mite
- the Outer God
- the deeper village ritual
- the complete relationship between the Hero and Mite

### Final path

The Hero ultimately sides, intentionally or practically, with the rebel / restoration army against the Church.

The rebels defeat the Church and regain control of the Kingdom.

But the victory is not a true salvation.

The new rulers gradually become the next version of the same corrupt system.

The cycle continues.

### Theme

凡人之墓 = We are all mortal.

People struggle, overthrow one power, build another, and eventually repeat the same corruption.

There is no transcendent salvation.

There is no permanent victory.

There is no escape from mortality.

The ending should feel bittersweet rather than like a failure screen.

The player defeated the immediate enemy.

The player simply did not defeat the deeper human cycle.

---

## 8.2 Ending II — 永恒乐园

### Trigger concept

The Hero becomes deeply entangled with Mite.

He is seduced / convinced by her and ultimately **physically joins with her**.

This physical union is not merely romantic or sexual.

It is the biological / supernatural mechanism that makes Hero a viable vessel for the Outer God.

The union allows the Outer God's influence to enter, overwrite, or absorb Hero's individuality.

### Result

The Outer God successfully manifests / takes control of the world.

Humanity is no longer living ordinary human lives.

People fall into an eternal dream / eternal paradise.

The imagery should evoke:

- Eden
- Paradise Lost
- a world without ordinary suffering
- a beautiful dream that has eliminated individual separation

There is no ordinary human suffering in the old sense.

But there is also no meaningful individual freedom.

### Theme

永恒乐园 = escape from mortality by abandoning separateness.

This draws inspiration from the idea of collective human merging represented by Evangelion's Human Instrumentality, but the Nereis version must be its own mythology. Evangelion's themes strongly involve individuality, consciousness, freedom, choice and responsibility, while the Instrumentality concept involves merging human consciousness into a single existence. 

### Eden inversion

Genesis presents humanity in Eden, describes man and woman becoming "one flesh", and presents the Tree of Life as the means by which humanity could live forever; after the Fall, access to that tree is guarded by a flaming sword. 

Nereis can invert that structure:

- Hero + Mite become literally one flesh.
- Their union opens access to the Outer God.
- "Paradise" becomes attainable.
- Eternal life is purchased by surrendering individual humanity.

The goal is not to retell Genesis. It is to turn its symbols into cosmic-horror machinery.

### Mite's temptation

Mite should not tempt Hero with power.

She should tempt him with **relief**:

> You have suffered enough.
>
> You do not need to remember.
>
> You do not need to die.
>
> You do not need to lose anyone again.
>
> You can stay with me.
>
> Nothing has to hurt anymore.

The player should understand why Hero might accept.

The paradise should be beautiful.

That beauty is part of the horror.

---

## 8.3 Ending III — 烈火天罚

### Trigger concept

The Hero also becomes physically joined with Mite.

However, unlike Ending II, the Hero is not completely consumed.

Earlier exploration matters.

The player must have discovered several hidden side stories and assembled a **multi-part key artifact**.

The exact pieces and side stories are not fixed yet.

### Design inspiration

The structural inspiration is Bloodborne's Third Umbilical Cord system: the important lesson is not the specific item, but that multiple seemingly independent discoveries can converge into a hidden endgame state. Bloodborne contains four versions of the relevant relic, while three are sufficient to unlock the alternate endgame; the associated "Insight" also changes how much of the hidden world the player can perceive. 

Nereis should use **multiple independent side stories**, not one hidden quest.

Each side story should feel unrelated on the first playthrough.

Only late in the game does the player realize that the pieces form one answer to the same problem:

> How can a human remain human after the vessel opens?

### Result

The Hero reaches the final truth but retains enough of himself to resist total submission.

The final conflict includes the Church's highest authority / final human antagonist.

The Hero ultimately chooses destruction over surrender.

The conclusion is fire / explosion / annihilation.

The Hero and Mite may die together.

The important thing is:

> **Hero chooses the end while still being himself.**

That distinction is the heart of 烈火天罚.

### Theme

烈火天罚 = knowledge does not save humanity; it gives one person the ability to make a final choice.

It is not a clean heroic victory.

The Hero does not restore the world.

He prevents one form of apocalypse by destroying what has already become irreparably corrupted.

---

# 9. Hero × Mite — The Emotional Core

A genuine Hero / Mite relationship is one of the emotional foundations of the game.

It should combine:

**love + innocence + manipulation + memory + dependency + cosmic horror**

The central question is not:

> Is Mite evil?

The central questions are:

> Did Mite genuinely love Hero?

> Did Hero love Mite before his memory was destroyed?

> If Mite does not understand human morality, can her love still be called real?

> If her love is real, does that make what she is doing more tragic — or more frightening?

> If Hero discovers the truth too late, can he still love her and choose to burn everything anyway?

This relationship is what makes the three endings emotionally different instead of merely mechanically different.

---

## 10. How Mite and Hero Should Interact

Mite should not appear constantly.

She should become more important as the Hero's emotional life grows.

### Early

- She helps him recover.
- She is curious about him.
- She asks ordinary questions.
- She notices tiny details about his behavior.
- She is fascinated by the fact that his body remembers combat but his mind does not.
- She gives him normal human experiences after the Church's experimentation.

### Middle

- The Hero begins remembering fragments that do not match Mite's version of events.
- Mite knows things she should not know.
- She sometimes disappears when Church-related clues appear.
- The player begins to suspect her before Hero does.
- Mite may show genuine hurt when Hero investigates the Church or leaves the village.

### Late

- Mite's relationship with Hero becomes undeniable.
- She still never gives Hero a clean exposition dump about her true identity.
- She begins asking Hero to stay.
- Her requests become increasingly difficult to interpret as either romance or manipulation.
- The player understands more than Hero does.

### Endgame

Mite's temptation must be personal.

She should speak to Hero's wounds, not his ambition.

The final emotional choice should feel like:

**love versus humanity**, not **good versus evil**.

---

# 11. The Outer God

### Current direction

The Outer God should be closer to Lovecraftian cosmic indifference than to a conventional Satan figure.

Cosmicism is useful here because humanity's terror comes from discovering that the universe does not revolve around human meaning, and that cosmic entities may have no human moral category such as good or evil. 

The current preferred direction is:

> The Outer God does not hate humanity.
>
> It does not particularly want the Kingdom.
>
> It does not need revenge.
>
> Humanity is simply interesting.

The relationship can be closer to:

> A human watches ants move a leaf.
>
> The human becomes curious.
>
> The human puts a finger in their path.
>
> The ants react.
>
> The human watches.

The difference is that the "finger" in this story is a cosmic being.

### Why the eternal dream exists

A crucial design possibility is that the Outer God is fascinated by the human desire to escape pain, death and loneliness.

So it creates the ultimate experiment:

> **What happens if every human desire for connection is granted completely?**

The answer is the eternal dream.

Everyone can be together.

No one has to die.

No one has to be alone.

No one has to suffer.

But no one can remain fully separate either.

This combines the thematic direction of Instrumentality with the indifference of cosmicism while remaining a distinct Nereis idea. 

### Important rule

The Outer God should not deliver long speeches explaining this.

Its motives should be inferred from:

- strange rituals
- recurring symbols
- dreams
- Mite's innocent comments
- ancient records
- environmental changes
- the consequences of the final union

The final answer should remain somewhat beyond human comprehension.

---

# 12. Sacred Union / Physical Combination

The confirmed direction is:

**the combination is physical.**

It is an actual bodily union between Hero and Mite and it functions as the vessel-opening mechanism.

This gives the story a strong mythic basis: in Genesis, the union of man and woman is described as becoming "one flesh." Nereis can transform that familiar phrase into literal cosmic-horror mechanics. 

The broader mythological idea of sacred union also exists in the concept of *hieros gamos*, where divine-human or divine-divine union can symbolically or ritually connect earthly and divine power. 

**Nereis should not copy those myths.**

The production idea is:

> Human intimacy becomes the physical gate through which the incomprehensible enters the human world.

This makes the final relationship simultaneously:

- romantic
- intimate
- spiritual
- biological
- horrific

# 13. The Four-Part Hidden Artifact

The 烈火天罚 key should be built from **multiple independent side stories**.

The player should never receive a quest saying:

> "Collect four fragments to unlock the true ending."

Instead:

- one part comes from a Church experiment victim
- one part comes from a forgotten village story
- one part comes from a rebel / outsider story
- one part comes from another hidden truth

Those are only director examples; exact identities are not canon yet.

Each piece should initially have its own meaning.

Only after the player has several pieces should the common pattern become visible.

The final artifact should represent:

> **something that keeps a human being singular when the vessel process tries to make them one with something else.**

Do not make it a generic "anti-evil talisman."

It should be specifically tied to:

- memory
- identity
- mortality
- individual will

The final object could ultimately be less a weapon and more a **humanity anchor**.

This is a major design opportunity and remains unresolved.

# 14. Church Final Antagonist — Current Options

This is intentionally unresolved.

Three strong possibilities remain:

### A. The Pope / High Priest

He is the direct embodiment of institutional corruption.

Strength:

- clear thematic enemy
- strong connection to Church research
- easy to understand politically

Risk:

- can become too conventional

### B. The King

The King knows the Church is corrupt.

He permits or uses it because he believes:

> If the Church collapses, the Kingdom collapses.

This makes him a tragic ruler rather than a simple villain.

Strength:

- strongest connection to 凡人之墓
- creates a direct political tragedy
- makes "saving the Kingdom" morally complicated

Risk:

- requires careful explanation of the King's relationship with the Church

### C. Two-stage truth

Human phase: the King or High Priest.

Second phase: the body is transformed through ancient-god power.

The final monster is not merely "he became evil."

It is the physical consequence of a person who believed he could use a power beyond humanity and remain human.

**Current recommendation:** keep this undecided until the Kingdom's political history is fully designed.

# 15. Factions

## Church

Not a simple evil faction.

It is:

- the old holder of faith
- the institution that discovered ancient-god power
- the hidden ruler of the Kingdom
- a protector to some ordinary people
- a monster to its victims

Individual Church characters can sincerely believe their actions are necessary.

## Rebel / Restoration Army

Not a simple good faction.

Its purpose is to overthrow the Church and restore political control of the Kingdom.

But victory can reproduce the same corruption.

## Other Kingdoms

People from other kingdoms may appear.

Some seek:

- true faith power
- Church knowledge
- ancient-god power
- political revenge
- survival

Some can become allies.

Some can become corrupted and hostile.

This expands the world beyond Church versus village.

## Village

The village is not completely innocent.

Some villagers are manipulated.

Some are desperate.

Some knowingly participate in rituals.

Some may genuinely believe the ritual will save them.

Some can become enemies without considering themselves evil.

# 16. Narrative Delivery Rules

The desired references are:

- Dark Souls
- Bloodborne
- Blasphemous 2
- The Song of Saya
- Neon Genesis Evangelion
- Biblical Eden imagery
- Lovecraftian cosmic horror

These are **reference materials, not canon to be copied**.

### Rule 1 — No encyclopedia dumping

Do not create a lore menu that explains the entire setting up front.

### Rule 2 — Items are evidence

Weapons, armor, relics, keys, books, ritual objects, corpses, architecture and consumables can carry story fragments.

An item description should feel like evidence.

### Rule 3 — NPCs provide interpretation

NPCs should usually provide:

- rumors
- emotions
- partial memory
- bias
- lies
- misunderstandings
- personal motives

NPC dialogue should not become encyclopedia text.

### Rule 4 — Contradictions are allowed

Two sources can disagree.

This is especially important for:

- Church records
- village memories
- Mite's words
- Hero's memories

### Rule 5 — Environment is narrative

A laboratory, ruined chapel, burnt village, hidden room or abandoned battlefield should communicate part of the story without requiring dialogue.

### Rule 6 — The player should build theories

The player should be able to form a plausible but incomplete theory early, then discover evidence that forces them to revise it.

### Rule 7 — Mite must remain mysterious

Do not reveal Mite = Outer God's agent early through direct exposition.

The player should discover her nature gradually.

### Rule 8 — Hero must remain ignorant

Do not make Hero suddenly understand Mite's true nature just because the player has discovered it.

The dramatic irony depends on the information gap.

### Rule 9 — Romance must remain embedded in the world

Do not create a conventional "romance UI."

The relationship should be conveyed through:

- small acts of care
- repeated locations
- remembered habits
- objects
- songs
- pauses in dialogue
- changes in Mite's behavior
- what Hero chooses to protect

### Rule 10 — The three endings are interpretations, not routes

Do not turn the game into:

- Route A = Church
- Route B = Mite
- Route C = secret ending

The three endings should emerge from the accumulated state of the entire playthrough.

# 18. Important Story Mystery

The game should repeatedly return to one emotional contradiction:

> The Hero was sent by the Kingdom to investigate the village.
>
> The Church destroyed his identity because he learned too much.
>
> The village later saved him.
>
> The girl who saved him is the very being connected to the apocalypse.
>
> She genuinely loves him.
>
> He genuinely loves her without knowing what she is.
>
> The player knows enough to fear her.
>
> Neither love nor truth gives the player a clean solution.

The contradiction itself is the story.

# Latest Cross-Game Canon Clarification — 2026-10-11

This section supersedes earlier wording that made physical union the gateway to the three endings. Older notes preserve historical design context; this section is authoritative when they conflict.

## Ending selection is no longer relationship-gated

The Hero's three endings are determined by accumulated Resonance, truth-anchor items, and exploration. Physical intimacy with Mite is optional narrative content and is never read by the ending evaluator. Current prototype contract:

- Cosmic Resonance threshold: 68.
- Eternal Paradise: Resonance >= 68, Outer-God exposure >= 6, and at least one hidden region entered, unless Blazing Judgment requirements are met.
- Blazing Judgment: Resonance >= 68, at least 3 unique truth-anchor sources, and at least 2 hidden regions entered.
- Mortal's Grave: otherwise.

Implementation source of truth: scripts/core/ending_evaluator.gd. These are prototype tuning values, not immutable metaphysical laws.

## Shared-world cycle after Mortal's Grave

In Nereis-Hero, Hero defeats the Church's leadership and the King, helping the Restoration Army win. The political victory is real, but the Restoration Army gradually recreates the structures of coercion and corruption it claimed to destroy.

The planned Nereis-Lullaby cross-game mainline, primarily from Mite's perspective, follows Hero's later compromises and fall inside the new order until he abandons Mite; the world enters another cycle. He repeatedly chooses public duty and institutional survival over personal bonds. This must be a gradual character tragedy, not an unexplained personality flip. Other branches may explore alternatives.

The Outer God remains indifferent to these human cycles. It continues seeking another vessel even if Mite is abandoned or one vessel plan fails.

## Canon constraints

- Mite's early innocence is genuine.
- Hero does not consciously identify Mite as an Outer-God proxy during the RPG main story.
- Physical union may change relationship scenes and the influence Mite experiences, but is not required to reach any ending.
- The three endings are driven by Hero's Resonance, items and exploration.
- See CROSS_GAME_STORY_CANON_CN.md and CROSS_GAME_STORY_CANON_EN.md for the shared-world continuation.
