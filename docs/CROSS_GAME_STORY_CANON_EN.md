# Nereis Cross-Game Story Canon

> Status: Cross-game narrative baseline / 2026-10-11  
> Projects: Nereis-Hero (action/exploration RPG) and Nereis-Lullaby (novel-style game primarily from Mite's perspective).

## 1. One world, two forms of play

Nereis-Hero lets players inhabit the amnesiac knight and infer what happened through combat, interconnected box-world exploration, Resonance, Sight, item descriptions and environmental evidence. Nereis-Lullaby tells the same world's human story primarily through Mite: daily life, relationships, psychological change, choices, and the way people are consumed by history.

The games should not repeat the same scenes. The RPG presents external events and explorable evidence; the novel-style game fills in inner lives, the passage of time, and consequences after the RPG endings.

## 2. The Restoration Army and Mortal's Grave

In Nereis-Hero's Mortal's Grave ending, Hero defeats the Church's highest authority and the King, helping the Restoration Army remove the Church's immediate control. The victory is real: the Church's research machine is disrupted, the old rulers fall, and survivors gain room to breathe.

But the Restoration Army has not solved the problem of power. In the name of stability, it gradually rebuilds intelligence networks, military command, resource rationing and ideological control. Emergency measures become permanent; dissenters are labelled traitors; technologies left by the Church are repackaged as necessities for national security or restoration.

The corruption is gradual, not an overnight twist. It grows from the self-justification that only the new rulers can prevent the old disaster from returning. The new order slowly inherits the old Church's role.

## 3. Hero after the RPG

The end of Nereis-Hero means Hero has completed the immediate battle, not that he has escaped politics and power forever. The planned cross-game mainline in Nereis-Lullaby follows his later choices inside the Restoration Army's new order. He initially believes he is protecting survivors, preserving peace, or preventing the Kingdom's collapse. He then tolerates methods he once opposed, eventually becoming an enforcer or silent accomplice of the new regime, and abandons Mite. The world enters the next cycle.

This should not read as an abrupt transformation into a villain. A stronger arc is that he repeatedly places Mite, private promises and individual lives behind a supposedly greater duty, until he realizes he has become the kind of person he once hated. Other branches may explore different choices, but this cross-game mainline connects the RPG's political victory to the novel game's personal tragedy.

## 4. Mite and the Outer God

Mite's early innocence, curiosity and affection are genuine. She does not fully understand her connection to the Outer God and should not be reduced to a villain who manipulated Hero from the beginning.

The Outer God has no human loyalty, love or political allegiance. It does not stop because Hero defeats the Church, the Restoration Army establishes a new state, or Mite is abandoned. Human regimes are temporary conditions; Mite is not the only possible vessel.

If one vessel plan fails, the Outer God continues searching for another suitable vessel. This cycle may be conveyed through dreams, recurring lullabies, similar rituals, forgotten research records or another anomalous person. Avoid explaining the entire truth in a single exposition dump.

## 5. Shared ending rules

Nereis-Hero's three endings are always selected by the Hero's Resonance, truth-anchor items and exploration:

- **Mortal's Grave**: the combined Resonance and exploration evidence for a cosmic ending is missing. Humanity wins politically, but corruption repeats.
- **Eternal Paradise**: Resonance becomes a signal the Outer God can answer; the player encounters an Outer-God trace but has not assembled enough truth anchors to preserve the Hero's selfhood. He becomes a vessel and the world enters an eternal dream.
- **Blazing Judgment**: Resonance is strong, Hero assembles at least three truth-anchor sources and explores deeply enough to preserve agency. He can resist the Outer God, but the cost remains catastrophic.

Physical intimacy with Mite may be an important relationship event, but it is not a prerequisite for any ending and must never block main-story completion.

## 6. Writing constraints

1. Hero does not explicitly learn Mite's true Outer-God identity during the RPG main story.
2. Mite's early innocence is not a deception.
3. Do not make the Restoration Army instantly evil after defeating the Church; corruption must grow through seemingly reasonable governance.
4. Nereis-Lullaby stays primarily in Mite's perspective and supplements her experience of Hero's later changes rather than replaying the RPG's combat.
5. The Outer God's search for a new vessel is a long-term threat beyond human political cycles.
6. Intimacy changes emotional context and character arcs, not the RPG's three-ending selector.
