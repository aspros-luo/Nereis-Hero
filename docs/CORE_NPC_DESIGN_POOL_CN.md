# Nereis Hero — Core NPC Design Pool v0.1

> Status: DESIGN BASELINE / NOT YET FULLY CANONICAL
>
> Purpose: establish the first layer of memorable NPCs before assigning exact dialogue, spawn states, regional placement, quests, enemy encounters, items, and scene implementation.
>
> Design principle:
>
> **Who → When → Where → What → Why**
>
> NPCs should be designed as characters first, then placed into the world. Do not turn every NPC into a quest giver.

---

## 1. NPC architecture

Nereis Hero uses four practical NPC layers:

### A. Core NPCs

Memorable characters who may cross multiple regions and whose personal arcs can change with Hero's progress and choices.

Current confirmed / proposed core pool:

- **Mite / 蜜忒**
- **金正**
- **外来朝圣骑士**
- 太阳骑士型角色 — candidate
- 帕奇型角色 — candidate
- 金闪闪型强者 — candidate

Core NPC does **not** mean frequent appearance. Narrative importance and screen time are separate variables.

### B. Regional important NPCs

Characters strongly tied to a region, faction, incident, or local history.

Examples to be designed later:

- forest hunter
- Kingdom deserter
- Restoration Army member
- Church cleric
- noble / court figure
- ruin survivor
- local witness

### C. Functional NPCs

NPCs whose primary role is gameplay support, while still receiving concise personal stories where appropriate.

Examples:

- blacksmith
- item merchant
- healer / rest-related NPC
- craftsperson
- apothecary

### D. World NPCs

Low-persistence characters who mainly make the world speak:

- villagers
- hunters
- farmers
- soldiers
- pilgrims
- traders
- travelers
- people beside recruitment notices

They should communicate rumors, fears, prejudice, misunderstandings, and daily life without becoming exposition terminals.

---

## 2. Village rule

The village is the early-game transfer and information hub, inspired by the functional role of Firelink Shrine / Dirtmouth rather than a conventional quest town.

Initial NPC count should remain small.

NPCs can gradually arrive, leave, die, relocate, or disappear as Hero explores.

Not every Core NPC returns to the village.

The village should feel like a place where the world slowly gathers around Hero, not a lobby containing every important character.

Village NPC dialogue should primarily reveal:

- rumors
- local concerns
- fragments of faction activity
- changes in the surrounding world
- personal opinions
- misleading interpretations

Examples of information types:

- beasts have become more aggressive in the forest
- the well water tastes wrong
- the Kingdom / Church occasionally posts conscription notices
- unknown people have appeared outside the village
- rumors about Restoration Army activity

NPCs generally **do not issue conventional quest chains** merely to deliver this information.

# 3. Core NPC — Mite / 蜜忒

## Role

**Core NPC / high narrative importance / intentionally low early-to-mid visibility**

## Design rule

Mite should feel almost optional for much of the RPG.

She is important to the deeper story, but the player should not immediately perceive her as a central plot character.

If the player does not pursue the **Eternal Paradise**-associated relationship/content direction, Hero may have relatively little sustained contact with her.

The desired retrospective feeling is:

> "She was there the whole time. I simply did not realize how important she was."

## Personality baseline

- genuinely innocent
- curious
- emotionally sincere
- not consciously plotting an apocalypse
- not a conventional seductress
- not initially aware of the Outer God's influence in a conscious human sense

Her innocence is genuine. It is not a disguise.

## Narrative function

Mite carries the emotional and cosmic contradiction at the center of the story:

- genuine affection
- human loneliness
- Outer-God influence
- the possibility of Eternal Paradise
- the tragedy of losing individual identity

## Visibility rule

Do not overuse Mite.

Her absence is part of her characterization.

## Ending rule

Physical intimacy / relationship content is optional narrative content and does not directly determine the ending evaluator.

Current ending selection remains based on Resonance, truth-anchor items, and exploration.

# 4. Core NPC — 金正

## Role

**Core NPC / social wildcard / antagonist-capable opportunist**

## Core definition

金正 is a synthesis of two reference archetypes:

- the charisma, pride, and confidence of a Gilgamesh-like character
- the opportunism, manipulation, survival instinct, and theatrical social behavior of a Patches-like character

However, he is **not a copy of either**.

His defining principle is:

> **利益优先。**

He is a genuine utilitarian opportunist.

He does not need to hate someone to hurt them.

He does not need to love someone to help them.

He asks:

> **"这件事对我有什么好处？"**

If the answer is favorable, he helps.

If the answer is unfavorable, he may betray.

If killing a weak NPC improves his position, he can kill that NPC without hesitation.

If betraying Hero is more profitable than helping Hero, he can betray Hero without remorse.

## Personality keywords

- 利己主义
- 聪明
- 能屈能伸
- 擅长观察
- 擅长说服
- 有自信
- 有强烈的利益判断
- 对强者有现实主义式尊重
- 对弱者缺少道德负担
- 很会逃跑
- 不会为了所谓面子做无意义的死战

## Critical characterization rule

金正**不是一个“其实是好人”的角色**。

Do not later reveal a tragic childhood that retroactively excuses everything.

He may have reasons, but reasons are not redemption.

He can sincerely help Hero and still later betray him.

He can admire Hero and still harm Hero.

He can respect a powerful enemy and still plot against them if an opportunity appears.

## Relationship with strength

Against someone clearly stronger:

- he can become polite
- he can flatter
- he can retreat
- he can cooperate temporarily

This is not cowardice in a comedic sense.

It is survival intelligence.

A recurring player reaction should be:

> "这家伙怎么这么没骨气？"

followed later by:

> "……不对，他又活下来了。"

## Relationship with Hero

His behavior toward Hero should be state-dependent rather than affection-dependent.

Possible states:

| Situation | Likely behavior |
|---|---|
| Hero weak / has little leverage | manipulate |
| Hero owns something valuable | flatter / cooperate |
| Hero can advance his interests | actively help |
| Hero threatens his interests | deceive / sabotage |
| Hero becomes overwhelmingly strong | temporary respect / cooperation |
| 金正 is cornered | negotiate / surrender / escape |
| 金正 regains leverage | may retaliate |

## Player trust goal

The intended player reaction is:

> **"I never know whether 金正's next sentence is true."**

Seeing him should make the player inspect the situation for a trap.

## No conventional morality meter

Do not reduce him to a good/evil slider.

His behavior should emerge from:

- leverage
- information
- risk
- reward
- Hero's current strength
- faction situation
- NPC usefulness

## Possible long-form arc

A candidate progression:

1. charming and useful
2. suspiciously opportunistic
3. first clear lie
4. first visible exploitation of a weaker NPC
5. unexpected genuine assistance to Hero
6. serious betrayal
7. temporary reconciliation for mutual benefit
8. final unresolved relationship

The exact sequence is not canon yet.

# 5. Core NPC — 外来朝圣骑士

## Role

**Core NPC / traveling character / optimistic counterweight**

## Character reference

The intended emotional reference is the broad archetype of a "Siegmeyer-like" wandering knight:

- justice-oriented
- genuinely heroic
- hearty
- straightforward
- honorable
- somewhat clumsy
- easily lost
- occasionally overconfident

He should be an original character, not a direct copy.

## Core personality

- 正义感强
- 豪迈
- 乐观
- 真诚
- 有骑士礼仪
- 自来熟
- 严重路痴
- 对危险的判断偶尔离谱
- 实力不弱，但没有自己想象得那么无敌

## Critical rule

He is **not a joke NPC**.

His clumsiness and directionlessness are the player's entry point to liking him.

His real dramatic value comes from:

> **He actually believes in doing the right thing.**

He should never become a lore encyclopedia.

As an outsider, he can naturally provide a limited external perspective on the Kingdom and its reputation, but he should primarily speak from personal experience.

## Initial premise

He came to the Kingdom as an outsider on pilgrimage.

His immediate practical problem can be simple and memorable:

> **He is trying to find the correct road to the holy destination and keeps getting lost.**

This can become a recurring joke without reducing him to comedy.

## Desired relationship with Hero

He should not be Hero's mentor.

He is another person moving through the same broken world.

Hero may become more cynical as he discovers the Church and Kingdom's corruption; the pilgrim can initially retain a stronger belief in justice.

Their values can occasionally clash.

## Candidate arc

1. first encounter while lost
2. second encounter after he has unexpectedly helped ordinary people
3. increasing doubts about the Kingdom's holy institutions
4. discovery that the pilgrimage destination is not what he believed
5. choice between comfortable belief and painful truth
6. possible heroic or tragic conclusion depending on the final design

Exact death, survival, and ending are intentionally unresolved.

## Desired player emotion

The target is not:

> "This NPC is funny."

It is:

> **"I hope this idiot makes it."**

# 6. Candidate core archetypes still needed

These are not yet canon.

## 6.1 Solar / idealist archetype

Purpose:

- represent uncomplicated faith or idealism
- become a bright contrast to Hero's increasingly dark worldview
- potentially develop a meaningful failure or sacrifice

Need to avoid simply reproducing Solaire.

## 6.2 Patches-like archetype

This is now partially absorbed by 金正.

Do not create a second character who performs exactly the same social function unless a strong contrast is found.

## 6.3 Gilgamesh-like powerful narcissist

This is also partially absorbed by 金正, but the two should not be treated as identical.

If a separate powerful character is created, their core should be:

> **absolute self-worth / overwhelming strength / contempt for ordinary limits**

rather than opportunism.

# 7. NPC design workflow

For every important NPC, fill the following before implementation:

### WHO

- identity
- faction
- age / adult status where relevant
- personality
- appearance
- first impression
- core desire
- core flaw
- personal contradiction
- relationship with Hero

### WHEN

- first appearance
- subsequent appearances
- progress gates
- reaction to Hero's major decisions
- death / departure timing
- end-state

### WHERE

- first region
- later regions
- village presence or absence
- relocation rules
- encounter conditions

### WHAT

- what the player sees
- what the NPC says
- what the NPC does
- gameplay function, if any
- item interactions
- combat interactions
- consequences

### WHY

- why the NPC is there
- why they help / refuse / betray
- why they move
- what they misunderstand
- what they know
- what they do not know
- what they want the player to believe

# 8. Production rules

1. Do not make every NPC a quest giver.
2. Do not make every NPC morally gray; strong contrasts are valuable.
3. Do not overpopulate the village.
4. Do not reveal every character's hidden truth immediately.
5. Let NPCs change location as the world changes.
6. NPC deaths should have causes and consequences.
7. NPC dialogue should carry bias and incomplete information.
8. A memorable NPC should have at least one behavior that players can recognize without reading their name.
9. Functional NPCs should still feel like people.
10. Do not implement a major NPC arc until WHO / WHEN / WHERE / WHAT / WHY is sufficiently specified.
11. Do not silently invent unresolved canon.
12. Reference characters are inspiration only; final characters must have their own motivations, relationships, and outcomes.

---

# 9. Current confirmed design decisions

- Mite is a core NPC but should have intentionally low early/mid-game presence.
- Mite becomes substantially more relevant if the player pursues the Eternal Paradise-associated content.
- 金正 is a core NPC.
- 金正 is fundamentally self-interested and can help, exploit, betray, or kill based on advantage.
- 金正 is capable of killing weak NPCs without moral hesitation if it benefits him.
- 金正 can combine charisma, pride, manipulation, opportunism, and survival intelligence.
- 金正 must not be retroactively "secretly good."
- An outsider pilgrim knight exists in the setting.
- The pilgrim knight is intended as a justice-oriented, hearty, straightforward, somewhat lost outsider.
- The pilgrim knight is inspired by the broad emotional role of a Siegmeyer-like character, not a direct copy.
- The village is an early transfer / information hub rather than a conventional quest town.
- NPCs should be designed globally before being assigned exact locations and schedules.

## 11. Implementation boundary

This document is a design baseline.

**Do not yet add full NPC logic to gameplay scenes.**

The appropriate next implementation step is to create the NPC data/registry structure only after the core pool is sufficiently settled.

Once the core pool is approved, implementation can introduce:

- stable NPC IDs
- progress-state identifiers
- spawn-location identifiers
- appearance conditions
- dialogue-state identifiers
- death / relocation states
- relationship flags where genuinely required

Avoid hard-coding narrative logic into individual scene scripts when a data-driven state model can represent it cleanly.
